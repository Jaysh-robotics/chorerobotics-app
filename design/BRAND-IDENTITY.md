## 1. Brand Foundation

### Brand Purpose

To liberate homeowners from the burden of repetitive outdoor labor, giving them back their time while their property receives professional-grade care through autonomous precision.

### Brand Vision

A world where every outdoor chore is handled by an intelligent, tireless machine -- where Saturday mornings belong to families, not to lawnmowers, snow shovels, or leaf blowers.

### Brand Mission

ChoreRobotics builds the most versatile autonomous outdoor robot, combining lawn mowing, snow plowing, and leaf collection in a single machine that delivers industrial-grade precision to residential properties without wires, without labor, without compromise.

### Brand Values

1. **Precision**: Every pass is calculated, every boundary respected, every blade of grass or inch of snow addressed with centimeter-level accuracy. We obsess over the details so our customers don't have to.

2. **Reliability**: Built to ISO 26262 safety standards and engineered for four-season outdoor operation. When ChoreBot is assigned a task, it completes it -- rain, cold, or heat.

3. **Autonomy**: True set-and-forget operation. Wire-free boundary mapping, RTK GPS navigation, and intelligent path planning mean the robot thinks for itself after initial setup.

4. **Versatility**: One machine, three seasons of outdoor work. Swap attachments, not robots. Mow in summer, clear snow in winter, collect leaves in autumn.

5. **Transparency**: Real-time telemetry, honest battery estimates, clear diagnostic reporting. The owner always knows exactly what ChoreBot is doing and why.

### Brand Promise

Your yard, handled. Every season. Every time.

## 2. Brand Archetype & Personality

### Primary Archetype: The Ruler-Creator Hybrid

ChoreRobotics occupies the intersection of two archetypes:

- **The Ruler**: Authority, control, mastery over the environment. The robot commands the yard with precise, deliberate movements. It projects competence and order.
- **The Creator**: Innovation, engineering excellence, building something that did not exist before. The technology is novel and the product category is being defined.

### Brand Personality Traits

| Trait | Description | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **Capable** | Confident without arrogance. The robot handles complex terrain and weather because it was engineered to, not because it is showing off. | Direct language, strong verbs, no hedging. "ChoreBot mows your lawn" not "ChoreBot can help with your lawn." |
| **Precise** | Obsessive attention to detail at the engineering level. Centimeter-level GPS. Every data point surfaced. | Monospaced numerics in the UI. Exact percentages, never vague. Data-forward communication. |
| **Rugged** | Built for the real outdoors -- mud, snow, rain, hills. Not a delicate gadget. Industrial-grade materials, all-terrain tires, weatherproof construction. | Dark, grounded visual language. Heavy typeface weights. Imagery that shows the robot in real conditions. |
| **Intelligent** | The robot makes decisions. It plans paths, detects obstacles, manages its own battery. It earns trust through autonomous competence. | Clean interface design. Information hierarchy that respects the user's attention. Proactive status updates. |
| **Reliable** | The single most important trait. If the robot is supposed to mow on Tuesday at 8am, it mows on Tuesday at 8am. | Consistent visual language. No decorative variation. Same patterns repeated with discipline across every screen. |

### Core Brand Tension

**"Industrial precision meets outdoor freedom."**

This tension is the engine of the brand. The robot is an industrial machine -- sensors, motors, safety systems, telemetry -- but it exists to create freedom. Freedom from chores. Freedom to enjoy your property. The brand holds both of these truths simultaneously: the rigor of robotics and the ease of a well-kept yard you didn't have to maintain.

> **Note**: The following concept directions were developed before the logo was finalized and are retained for reference. The official logo above takes precedence.

### Concept A: "The Precision Mark"

**Description**: A geometric wordmark where "CHORE" is set in a custom-modified heavy grotesque with squared terminals, and "ROBOTICS" is set smaller beneath it in a wider-tracked weight. The distinctive feature is a precision crosshair element integrated into the letter "O" in CHORE -- a thin-stroke circle with orthogonal tick marks at 0/90/180/270 degrees, referencing GPS targeting and the robot's camera sensor. The crosshair subtly replaces the counter (interior) of the O.

**Icon variant**: The crosshair-O extracted as a standalone mark. A squared circle with four tick marks. At small sizes, it reads as a targeting reticle. At large sizes, the geometric precision of the construction is visible.

**Color application**: Primary use is white mark on dark backgrounds. The crosshair tick marks use the brand amber (#D4A012) as an accent, echoing the robot's amber LED accents.

**Rationale**: Directly references the product's RTK GPS precision. The squared letterforms echo the robot's boxy chassis geometry. The crosshair is a universal symbol for "locked on target" -- communicating reliability and accuracy.

### Concept B: "The Terrain Logotype"

**Description**: A bold, all-caps logotype "CHOREROBOTICS" with a horizontal line running through the baseline of the text -- a continuous ground plane that extends slightly beyond the first and last characters. This ground line has a subtle undulation (one shallow sine wave) representing terrain. Below the ground line, a small chevron pointing downward sits centered, representing the blade/plow making contact with the surface.

**Icon variant**: The ground-line-and-chevron extracted as a standalone mark. A horizontal line with a single terrain undulation and a downward chevron at center. Minimal and distinctive.

**Color application**: Logotype in white. Ground line in brand amber (#D4A012). Chevron in primary blue (#0078FF). The two-accent approach mirrors the robot's amber LEDs and the app's blue UI accent.

**Rationale**: Communicates the "outdoor" half of the brand tension. The ground plane is literal -- this robot works on the ground, on real terrain. The chevron is a geometric abstraction of a blade, plow, or collection mechanism. It says "we make contact with the earth."

### Concept C: "The Shield Monogram"

**Description**: An angular shield shape -- not rounded and friendly, but faceted and industrial, echoing the robot's chamfered body panels. Inside the shield, the letters "CR" are stacked in a monogram using a condensed geometric typeface. The shield's top edge has a thin amber line running across it, directly referencing the robot's distinctive horizontal LED light bar visible in all product photos.

**Icon variant**: The shield with monogram is already icon-ready. At 16px, the "CR" monogram simplifies to two vertical strokes differentiated by a crossbar on the R.

**Color application**: Shield fill is charcoal (#151820). Light bar accent in amber (#D4A012). Monogram letters in white (#E8EAED). On light backgrounds, the shield inverts to white with dark letters.

**Rationale**: The shield communicates protection and authority (the Ruler archetype). The faceted shape directly mirrors the robot's industrial design. The light bar reference creates an unmistakable visual connection between logo and product.

### Logo Usage Guidelines (Applicable to All Concepts)

**Clear space**: Minimum clear space around the logo equals the height of the "C" in CHORE on all sides.

**Minimum sizes**:
- Full wordmark: 120px wide minimum (digital), 30mm wide minimum (print)
- Icon mark: 16px minimum (digital), 5mm minimum (print)

**Color restrictions**:
- Primary: White mark on dark backgrounds (#0A0C10 through #1A1E28)
- Secondary: Dark mark (#0A0C10) on light backgrounds (#F0F2F5 and lighter)
- Never place the logo on busy photographic backgrounds without a solid backing
- Never alter the amber accent color
- Never rotate, stretch, add effects, or modify proportions

### Mode Switching Rules

1. Accent colors (blue, amber, task colors) remain the same hue in both modes. Only the 500-value shifts: dark mode uses the 500, light mode uses the 600 for WCAG contrast compliance.
2. Functional colors (success, warning, error) are identical across modes.
3. Safety colors (e-stop) never change under any circumstance.
4. Borders shift from white-alpha to black-alpha when switching modes.
5. Shadows intensify in light mode (higher opacity) and soften in dark mode (lower opacity, larger spread).

## 11. Typography System

### Typeface Selection

**Primary: Inter**
- **Usage**: All UI text, headings, body copy, navigation, buttons, labels
- **Weights used**: 400 (Regular), 500 (Medium), 600 (SemiBold), 700 (Bold), 800 (ExtraBold)
- **Rationale**: Inter was designed specifically for screen readability. Its tall x-height, open apertures, and carefully tuned optical sizes make it ideal for the data-dense robotics control interface. It has tabular number support, which is essential for telemetry displays.
- **Loading**: `Inter:wght@400;500;600;700;800`
- **Fallback stack**: `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, 'Segoe UI', Roboto, sans-serif`

**Monospace: JetBrains Mono**
- **Usage**: Telemetry values, diagnostic data, timestamps, coordinates, firmware versions, motor speeds, battery percentages, any numeric readout
- **Weights used**: 400 (Regular), 500 (Medium)
- **Rationale**: JetBrains Mono is a monospaced typeface with excellent digit differentiation (particularly 0/O and 1/l disambiguation). Its slightly condensed width allows dense telemetry grids without horizontal overflow. The ligature support is disabled in our implementation to ensure character-level readability.
- **Loading**: `JetBrains Mono:wght@400;500`
- **Fallback stack**: `'SF Mono', 'Fira Code', 'Cascadia Code', 'Consolas', monospace`
- **OpenType settings**: `font-feature-settings: 'liga' 0;` (ligatures off)

### Typography Rules

1. **Never use Inter below 10px.** At that size, switch to system font for OS-level rendering optimization.

When multiple cards or list items enter the viewport simultaneously, apply a stagger delay:

- **Stagger increment**: `50ms` per subsequent item

## 16. Brand Voice & Tone

### Voice Characteristics

The ChoreRobotics voice is the verbal expression of the robot's personality: capable, precise, and direct. It speaks like an expert colleague -- competent and clear, never patronizing, never uncertain.

**Capable**: Uses active voice and strong verbs. States what the robot does, not what it "helps with" or "tries to do." The robot mows. It clears. It collects. It returns.

**Precise**: Favors specifics over generalizations. "78% battery, approximately 2 hours 15 minutes remaining" rather than "battery is good." Numbers are exact. Times are stated. Distances are measured.

**Direct**: Gets to the point. No filler words, no corporate padding, no unnecessary adjectives. If the robot has an error, the app says "Motor fault detected. Blade stopped. Diagnostics available." Not "Oops! Something went wrong with your robot."

**Calm**: Never urgent except for genuine safety situations. The app projects quiet confidence. When something goes wrong, the tone is steady and informational, not panicked. When everything is working, the tone is quietly affirmative.

**Human but not cute**: The robot has a name (ChoreBot) and the app can reference it by name. But the app never anthropomorphizes the robot with emotions. ChoreBot doesn't "feel happy" or "want to help." ChoreBot is idle, active, or needs attention.

### Tone Spectrum

The voice stays consistent, but the tone shifts based on context:

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Normal operation | Calm, affirmative | "ChoreBot is mowing Zone 1. 45% complete." |
| Success / completion | Quietly satisfied | "Front yard complete. 2,400 sq ft in 47 minutes." |
| Warning / attention | Alert but measured | "Battery at 22%. Returning to dock." |
| Error / fault | Steady, informational | "Connection lost. Last known position: front yard. Reconnecting." |
| Safety / e-stop | Urgent, imperative | "EMERGENCY STOP ACTIVATED. All motors halted." |
| Onboarding / setup | Guiding, patient | "Place ChoreBot on the charging dock. The LED bar will turn amber when charging begins." |
| Empty / waiting | Inviting, brief | "No scheduled tasks. Tap Start to begin." |

### Writing Rules

1. **Lead with what, then why.** "Mowing paused -- obstacle detected ahead." Not "An obstacle was detected ahead, so mowing has been paused."
2. **Use sentence case everywhere.** Title Case is reserved for the brand name "ChoreRobotics" and the product name "ChoreBot" only. All UI labels, headings, and messages use sentence case.
3. **Keep notifications under 80 characters.** Push notifications are read on lock screens. Brevity is mandatory.
4. **Never use exclamation marks** except in the emergency stop context. Exclamation marks project either false excitement or unnecessary alarm.
5. **Avoid "smart" and "intelligent" in customer-facing copy.** These are expected of any autonomous robot. Instead, demonstrate intelligence through the specificity of the information shown.
6. **Use the metric system with imperial in parentheses** for the US market. For other markets, metric only.
7. **Time references are always specific.** "2 hours ago" not "recently." "Tuesday at 8:00 AM" not "next scheduled run."

### Vocabulary

**Preferred terms:**
- "ChoreBot" (the physical robot, always capitalized)
- "mow" / "mowing" (not "cut" or "trim")
- "clear" / "clearing" (for snow, not "plow" in user-facing copy)
- "collect" / "collecting" (for leaves, not "blow" or "vacuum")
- "zone" (a mapped area, not "area" or "section")
- "dock" (the charging station, verb and noun)
- "boundary" (the mapped perimeter, not "border" or "edge")
- "task" (a scheduled operation, not "job" or "chore")
- "idle" (waiting, not "sleeping" or "resting")
- "fault" (a diagnosed issue, not "problem" or "issue")

**Avoided terms:**
- "Smart" (too generic)
- "Easy" (too casual, undermines the product's engineering)
- "Just" (minimizing -- "just tap Start" implies the user might find it hard)
- "Oops" / "Uh oh" / "Yikes" (never)
- "Your little helper" or similar anthropomorphic diminutives
- "Sit back and relax" (cliche)

- Field photography: Robot on real grass, real snow, real leaves. Natural lighting, but maintain the premium feel. Shallow depth of field to keep focus on the robot.
